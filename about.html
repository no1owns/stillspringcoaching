ABOUT MIA

Hi, I'm Mia Feldbaum.

she/her

Coach

I believe disability and neurodivergence are natural parts of human diversity. I also know that navigating our needs, other people's expectations, and the systems around us can be complicated, whether or not we have any known conditions.

I'm interested in both the practical, everyday parts of life and the bigger questions we carry about who we are and how we think our lives are supposed to look.

I don't believe there's one right way to do things. I'm interested in helping people discover what works for them.

This work is personal to me.

I came to coaching through years of working in accessible and adaptive outdoor recreation, my own experiences with ADHD, and my family's experiences with neurodivergence and disability.

I'm not approaching this work as someone who has figured everything out and can show you how to do the same.

I'm interested in these questions, challenges, and possibilities because they're part of my life, too.

I know that systems and expectations that work beautifully for one person might not work for another. And sometimes the most useful thing we can do is question the expectation itself.

That's part of what draws me to coaching: making room for people to discover what they actually need, rather than trying harder to fit themselves into something that doesn't work.

What it's like to work with me

I listen, ask questions, and believe what you tell me about your experience. I'm not here to hand you a formula or tell you who you should become.

I'm here to help you get curious about what's true for you and what you might want to try next.

My background

My education and experiences inform how I think about disability, identity, social expectations, and the many different ways people experience the world.

I have a master's degree in Cultural Foundations of Education and a Certificate of Advanced Study in Disability Studies from Syracuse University, and a bachelor's degree in Anthropology from Rice University.

I'm also a certified California Naturalist and am currently completing coach training through The Coach Approach Training Institute. I've led wilderness trips for people with and without disabilities, served on a search and rescue team, and now serve on the board of BORP Adaptive Sports and Recreation, where I previously worked for years.

Everyone deserves to be met with respect.

My coaching approach is neurodiversity-affirming, disability-affirming, and gender-affirming.

You don't need a diagnosis, a particular identity, or a perfectly articulated goal to begin a conversation.

I'm interested in understanding your experiences, respecting your autonomy, and working with you to explore what feels right for your life.

Curious about working together?

We can start with a short, no-pressure conversation. You're welcome to bring questions, ideas, or simply a sense that something could be different.
